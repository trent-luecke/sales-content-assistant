## Draft post

The moment a software demo actually lands isn't when I'm talking. It's when I go quiet.

I used to think a good demo meant walking a coach through every feature — the
reporting, the program builder, the lot. Nice tour. Completely forgettable.

So now I do something different. Early on, I stop presenting and just log in as an
athlete — the same app their players would actually open. "Here's your session.
Here's the video. Injured today? Tap here, swap the exercise, leave me a note."

And I watch the coach's face change. Because they've stopped looking at a sales tool.
They're looking at the thing their athletes will hold in their hands every single day.

Then I shut up and ask: "How do your lot actually train — on their phones, or off a
screen in the gym?"

That's the whole job, honestly. Not selling the platform. Just showing a coach their
own world, running a little smoother.

If you're still figuring out what tech actually fits your setup — no worries at all.
Happy to just show you the athlete's-eye view. That's usually where it clicks.
